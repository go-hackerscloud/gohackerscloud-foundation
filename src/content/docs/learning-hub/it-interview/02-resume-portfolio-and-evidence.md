## Core technical and professional concepts

### Fundamental principle

Use problem, action, outcome and verification in project bullets. Never manufacture revenue, employer achievements or security metrics.

### Practical distinctions

A self-directed lab can demonstrate learning if clearly described as such, with assumptions, configurations and sanitized results.

### How teams use it

A portfolio README should cover scope, architecture, risks, test method, results, limitations and reproducibility.

### How to show evidence

Remove real customer data, hostnames, internal credentials, private tickets and sensitive logs from public examples.

## Real interview scenario

A candidate has reviewed a deliberately broad IAM policy. Instead of writing 'expert AWS architect', they describe the policy review, scoped replacement and two positive/negative tests.

**Answer structure:** restate the requirement; identify scope, constraints and risk; choose a test or decision; verify evidence; communicate the outcome.

## Guided preparation exercise

1. Rewrite one resume bullet in problem-action-evidence form.
2. Prepare a sanitized diagram and README.
3. Identify three claims you can defend without notes.
4. Draft a concise personal introduction.

Use only your own equipment, public documentation and authorized or synthetic environments. If you have only studied a technology, describe it accurately as study experience.

## Questions and suggested answers

1. **Can a simulated project be presented as a customer engagement?**

2. **What makes evidence stronger than a screenshot?**

   <details><summary>Show a model answer</summary>A clear requirement, repeatable tests, observed results and limitations.</details>


## Deliverable

Two evidence-based resume bullets and a portfolio README.

In a short write-up, include what you know, what you tested, what you inferred and what you could not verify.
