## Before the lab

Foundation lessons are public and free. Hosted environments are part of the separate GoHackersCloud Academy offering and require whatever access the specific track and membership provide. **Read the lab-specific scope and safety notice before running any security test.**

### 1 — Select a learning track

Open the [Academy tracks directory](https://academy.gohackerscloud.com/tracks/) and choose a topic aligned with your existing knowledge. A learner new to Linux should study file and process basics before attempting advanced assessment labs.

### 2 — Review the guide

For each lab, identify the objective, prerequisites, intended target, environment type, expected observations and completion criteria. If a lesson has no provisioned lab, use its reading or practice instructions; a track alone does not guarantee one.

### 3 — Authenticate and launch

Follow the official Academy access and membership process. For supported lessons, the platform can provision an isolated practice environment and expose appropriate terminal or desktop tools. If launch is unavailable, follow the documented support process rather than attempting to bypass access controls.

### 4 — Observe, reason and record

Record only what the learning exercise requires: commands, screenshots of synthetic lab targets, observed results, a short interpretation and suggested remediation. Never paste real tokens, customer data or live-system credentials into shared material.

### 5 — End the session

Use the intended End Lab workflow. Hosted environments may be temporary; do not treat a lab container as permanent storage. Keep allowed notes and learning artifacts on your own system.

## Example: Web security practice workflow

1. Read the OWASP topic and identify the security assumption being tested.
2. Select an intentionally vulnerable target provided in the authorized exercise.
3. Perform the safe, scoped observation steps from the lesson.
4. Record the evidence, expected security control and remediation concept.
5. End the session and review what you learned.

## Example: Cloud security practice workflow

1. Review AWS identity concepts and the account/resource scope.
2. Use the lab's documented simulated or authorized environment.
3. Identify a least-privilege, logging or network configuration issue.
4. Describe the business impact and a verification method.
5. Prepare a short report rather than repeating commands without analysis.

## Common support checklist

Check browser/session state, whether the lesson includes a launch control, whether your subscription permits the track, and whether a previous session needs to be ended. Share non-sensitive errors with official support. Do not share authentication cookies or access tokens.
