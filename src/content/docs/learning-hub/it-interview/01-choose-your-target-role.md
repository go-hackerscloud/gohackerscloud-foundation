## Core technical and professional concepts

### Fundamental principle

Compare service desk, cloud support, SOC analyst and junior security engineering responsibilities. Roles differ in authority, escalation, tool access and expected troubleshooting depth.

### Practical distinctions

Read three job descriptions. Separate must-have skills from preferred skills and identify what you can explain, practise and prove.

### How teams use it

Use an evidence matrix: skill, personal experience or simulation, artifact, gap and next learning action.

### How to show evidence

An interview is a professional discussion about judgments and capabilities. It is acceptable to describe what you do not yet know.

## Real interview scenario

A cloud support opening mentions Linux, AWS, DNS, scripting and IAM. The candidate prioritizes network diagnosis and permissions, then chooses one synthetic case study to support each skill.

**Answer structure:** restate the requirement; identify scope, constraints and risk; choose a test or decision; verify evidence; communicate the outcome.

## Guided preparation exercise

1. Create a matrix from three suitable job advertisements.
2. Rank the five most common technical requirements.
3. Write a clear 45-second introduction.
4. Set a two-week practice plan for your biggest gap.

Use only your own equipment, public documentation and authorized or synthetic environments. If you have only studied a technology, describe it accurately as study experience.

## Questions and suggested answers

1. **Can you claim a tool you have only read about?**

   <details><summary>Show a model answer</summary>Say you understand the concept but have not operated it; explain how you would test it safely.</details>

2. **What makes a good interview story?**

   <details><summary>Show a model answer</summary>Specific context, action taken by you, verified result and honest limitations.</details>


## Deliverable

A role-specific interview plan and a transparent skills matrix.

In a short write-up, include what you know, what you tested, what you inferred and what you could not verify.
