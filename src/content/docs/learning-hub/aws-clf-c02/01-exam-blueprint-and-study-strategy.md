**Track:** AWS Cloud Practitioner CLF-C02 · **Level:** Foundation · **Suggested study time:** 30–50 minutes

CLF-C02 is a foundational knowledge examination rather than a hands-on administrator test. Anchor your plan to AWS's official exam guide and practice recognizing which AWS service or concept fits a business need.

[Back to the complete AWS Cloud Practitioner guide](/learning-hub/aws-clf-c02/)

## Concepts to learn

### The key idea

The four official domains are Cloud Concepts 24%, Security and Compliance 30%, Cloud Technology and Services 34%, and Billing, Pricing and Support 12% of scored content.

### What changes between services

Questions may be multiple-choice or multiple-response. Read how many answers are required before choosing; eliminate options that are valid AWS services but do not solve the stated requirement.

### Real-world decision

AWS's official guide currently identifies 50 scored and 15 unscored items; an overall scaled score of at least 700 is required. AWS may revise details, so consult its official guide near your exam date.

### How to evaluate it

A strong plan alternates understanding, service comparison, original scenario questions, and review of missed concepts rather than memorizing marketing definitions.

## Worked scenario

A learner scores poorly on cost questions despite knowing EC2, S3 and Lambda. Instead of rereading all AWS services, they build a mistake log by exam domain and revisit billing concepts deliberately.

**Exam thinking pattern:** identify the requirement first—availability, security, performance, data model, integration, operations or cost—then select the service that directly addresses it. Be wary of answer options that are real services but solve a different problem.

## Study and practical reinforcement

No paid AWS account is required for the conceptual exercises. If you choose to experiment in your own AWS account, review charges, apply least privilege, never publish access keys, and clean up resources.

1. Download or bookmark the official CLF-C02 exam guide.
2. Set a diagnostic assessment across all four domains.
3. Make a 'concepts I confuse' list with examples.
4. Set a study calendar and review mistakes twice weekly.

## Mini knowledge check

1. **Is a Cloud Practitioner expected to engineer production Kubernetes clusters?**

   <details><summary>Reveal explanation</summary>No. It validates overall AWS concepts and service selection, not detailed engineering implementation.</details>

2. **Which two domains collectively have the largest weight?**

   <details><summary>Reveal explanation</summary>Security and Compliance plus Cloud Technology and Services account for 64% of scored content.</details>

## Study deliverable

A diagnostic baseline, gap log, study schedule and 15 flashcards. Keep this artifact in your own notes; identify services you still confuse and repeat the reasoning without looking at definitions.
