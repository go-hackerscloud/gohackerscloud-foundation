## Core technical and professional concepts

### Fundamental principle

An asset has value; a vulnerability is a weakness; risk includes likely adverse outcomes and their impact.

### Practical distinctions

The CIA triad describes broad information security objectives.

### How teams use it

An alert is an observation requiring validation and context, not automatic proof of compromise.

### How to show evidence

Security incident work includes triage, evidence handling, containment decisions, communications and recovery.

## Real interview scenario

A privileged sign-in appears unusual. The analyst checks identity, MFA status, IP/device context, change ticket and correlated activity before drawing conclusions.

**Answer structure:** restate the requirement; identify scope, constraints and risk; choose a test or decision; verify evidence; communicate the outcome.

## Guided preparation exercise

1. Create a synthetic alert with five important fields.
2. Write a short risk statement.
3. Distinguish false positive from false negative.
4. Describe a safe escalation message.

Use only your own equipment, public documentation and authorized or synthetic environments. If you have only studied a technology, describe it accurately as study experience.

In a short write-up, include what you know, what you tested, what you inferred and what you could not verify.
